A question from 1859, answered in 2026

The Riemann Hypothesis says that the prime numbers are spread as evenly as they can be. For 167 years it was the most famous open problem in mathematics. This page is about what the answer looks like when you can touch it.

1859
Riemann states the hypothesis
All non-trivial zeros of the zeta function lie on one vertical line. Equivalently, the primes follow their average law with the smallest possible error.
1952
Weil turns it into a sign
André Weil shows the hypothesis is the same statement as the positivity of one quadratic form, with the primes on one side and the Gamma factor on the other. A question about infinitely many zeros becomes a question about a plus or minus sign.
June 2026
Suzuki writes the form as an operator
Masatoshi Suzuki of the Institute of Science Tokyo expresses Weil's form through the screw function as a self-adjoint operator on an interval. The paper is pure theory.
July 2026
VIDRAFT makes the operator computable
The first numerical realization of Suzuki's operator. Its spectrum is mapped, its constants are found in closed form, and the lowest eigenvalue for the zeta function is measured: positive.
20 November 2026
Alpöge and Claude announce the proof
A formal proof in Lean, 175,187 files, covering the zeta function and Dirichlet L-functions. Their route passes through the same positivity that Weil identified.
21 November 2026
The gauge goes live
VIDRAFT extends its operator to Dirichlet L-functions and opens it to everyone. Genuine objects stay positive. Imitations go negative. You can run it above.

What the gauge proves to your eyes

Genuine stays positive

The zeta function and Dirichlet L-functions of conductor 3, 4, 5 and 8 keep the needle above zero across the whole resolvable range.

Imitation goes negative

Flip the signs of the primes, give the Gamma factor the wrong parity, or drop the pole term. One change is enough and the form turns negative, converged to three digits.

The form knows arithmetic

The difference between a true L-function and a copy is invisible to the eye and decisive for the sign. That sensitivity is the content of the Riemann Hypothesis.

The Cyclic Phase Hypothesis

The spectrum of the operator follows a closed form. One number in it, the one half, has a story.

mu_k = log(k - 1/2) + gamma + log(pi/2)

VIDRAFT proposed that the one half is a phase that winds once around the boundary. The idea came from two features of the spectrum: the shifts of the eigenvalues sum to zero, and the even and odd modes follow a cycle.

The test was a sweep from Dirichlet to Neumann boundary conditions. The offset moved from minus one half to plus one half, an integer step of exactly one. The hypothesis was confirmed and the offset was identified as the boundary Maslov phase.

A law found along the way

At the ends of the interval the eigenfunctions vanish like the inverse square root of a logarithm, u(d) ~ (log(1/d))^(-1/2). The gauge measures it directly in the third tab. It is the optimal boundary rate of the logarithmic Laplacian, proved in 2024 by Hernández-Santamaría, López Ríos and Saldaña, observed here for the Weil operator.
